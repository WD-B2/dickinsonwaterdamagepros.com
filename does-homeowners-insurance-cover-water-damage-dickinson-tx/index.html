Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Dickinson, TX?

Coverage depends on how the water got in. This guide sorts sudden leaks, slow leaks and floods using Texas Department of Insurance guidance, so you know what to ask your agent.

By the Dickinson Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 13, 2026.

Call (844) 950-6900See all services

The short answer is that it depends on the cause. Homeowners policies are written around events, and water damage falls into three buckets that insurers treat very differently: sudden and accidental damage, gradual damage and flooding. Knowing which bucket your loss belongs in explains most of what happens when you call your agent.

This guide is general information and not a coverage opinion. Your policy language controls, and your insurer decides the claim. What follows is meant to help you ask better questions.

Sudden and accidental water damage

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner and renter policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, and its examples are a burst pipe, a toilet overflow and a broken washer hose. If mold develops on a damaged item as a result, that is covered too. A supply line that lets go on a Saturday afternoon and floods a hallway fits this description.

Coverage for the damage does not always extend to the failed part. A policy may pay for the wet flooring and drywall and not for the pipe or the washer. Read the language about the cause of loss and ask how repairs to the source are handled.

Gradual leaks and seepage

The same state guidance says policies usually do not cover damage from gradual leaks. A slow drip under a sink that rotted a cabinet over months, or seepage through a foundation wall, tends to fall here, and mold that grows from that kind of leak is also excluded. Insurers often describe this as a maintenance issue.

That distinction is why timing matters. If you notice a stain and wait, the insurer may argue the leak was ongoing before the claim. Report problems when you find them and keep dated photos.

Floods

Home policies do not cover floods, according to the Texas Department of Insurance. Flood damage from rising water requires a separate flood policy, and mold that comes from a flood is also not covered by a home policy. Floodwater includes bayou overflow, storm surge and water that rises from the street.

Wind-driven rain is a different question

Some policies cover rain that enters through an opening made directly by wind or hail, such as a roof torn by a storm. Flood water rising from the ground is separate. Ask your agent which description fits a storm loss before you assume either way.

Questions to ask your agent

  • Does my policy cover sudden and accidental discharge from plumbing and appliances?
  • How does it treat a leak that was ongoing before I found it?
  • Do I have a flood policy, and what is its deductible?
  • Is sewer or drain backup covered, or does it need an endorsement?
  • Does the policy pay for temporary housing if I cannot stay home?

What to do before you file

Stop the source of water, protect the property from further damage and photograph everything. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, and keep receipts for emergency work. Cleanup that prevents more damage is something insurers generally expect. If you are in doubt about whether to proceed with drying while the claim is pending, ask the adjuster on the first call.

Our water extraction and structural drying pages explain the work that crews do in the first days, and the records they keep can support your file.

Deductibles and small claims

Many homeowners hesitate to file because of the deductible. A small leak that costs less than the deductible may not be worth a claim, though reporting it can still be wise because some policies treat unreported damage differently later. Ask your agent how filing a claim might affect your premium or renewal before you decide.

Rebuilding and mortgage lenders

If you have a mortgage, the lender may be named on claim checks, which adds a step. Plan for that when you budget the repair. Keep copies of estimates, invoices and the dry readings from the restoration crew so the lender and the insurer see the same record.

A Dickinson note: flood policies and the 60-day window

Dickinson sits on Dickinson Bayou, and the Texas Department of Insurance says standard home policies exclude floods. For anyone with a federal flood policy, TDI says claims must be filed and losses documented within 60 days.

In the aftermath of Hurricane Harvey in 2017 the National Weather Service reported totals over 40 inches common across the region, so many local homeowners learned the difference between a home policy and a flood policy the hard way. Check your declarations page for a flood policy before the next storm and not after.

Dickinson insurance questions

How do I know whether I have flood insurance in Dickinson?

Look for a separate flood policy, often through the National Flood Insurance Program, in your paperwork or ask your agent. A homeowners policy does not include it. A mortgage lender may have required it if your property is in a mapped flood zone.

Is a burst pipe in a Dickinson home covered?

Many policies cover sudden pipe failures, according to Texas Department of Insurance guidance, and each policy has its own terms. Report it promptly and keep the failed section if you can.

Does insurance pay for emergency drying?

Often policies expect you to protect the property from further damage and may reimburse reasonable emergency work. Ask your adjuster and keep receipts.

Where can I check flood risk for my address?

The FEMA flood map for your address shows the mapped zone, and your insurer or agent can explain what it means for coverage.
