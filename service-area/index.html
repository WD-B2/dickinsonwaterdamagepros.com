Where we dispatch around Dickinson

Dickinson Water Damage Pros is based at 3410 Gulf Freeway in Dickinson, and calls to the 24/7 line go to crews who work the Galveston Bay side of the Gulf Freeway corridor. Distance matters in emergency work, because standing water does not wait for a long drive, so this page lists the nearby towns we serve and the ZIP codes that go with them.

Each town below has its own page. They are not copies of one another. League City, Bacliff, Santa Fe, Kemah and Webster have different utilities, different permit offices and different flood histories, and the pages explain the differences that affect a restoration job.

Why county lines matter for restoration

Dickinson, League City, Bacliff, Santa Fe and Kemah are in Galveston County, and Webster is in Harris County. When a loss is large enough to need rebuilding permits, the permit office depends on the address. Incorporated cities run their own building departments, while unincorporated areas such as Bacliff fall under Galveston County for floodplain permits. The town pages name the right office for each place.

Water and sewer providers also differ from one address to the next. A municipal utility district, a water control and improvement district or the city itself may supply your water, which affects who you call when a main breaks or a meter has to be shut off. Ask your provider if the bill does not say.

What to tell dispatch about your address

When you call, give the street address first, then the town and the nearest cross street. If your mailing address uses one town name and the property sits in another, say so, because the two are not always the same on the ground. Tell the dispatcher whether the building is a house, a duplex, an apartment or a business, and which floor the water is on.

Mention anything that affects access. A gated community, a locked meter room or a long private drive can slow a crew down, and a quick note lets them plan. If you know where the main shutoff valve is, say that too. A crew that arrives knowing the water is already off can start extraction sooner.

If your town is not listed

Water does not follow a service map. If your address is in a nearby community that does not have its own page here, the line is still worth calling. Tell the dispatcher where you are and what happened, and they will say honestly whether a crew can reach you and roughly when. Being told no quickly is better than waiting on a promise that cannot be kept.

What to do before the crew arrives

Wherever you are in the area, the first steps are the same, and they follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

  • Shut off the main water valve if it is safe to reach.
  • Stay out of rooms where water touches outlets or appliances.
  • Do not wade through standing water of unknown source.
  • Photograph the damage before anything is moved.
