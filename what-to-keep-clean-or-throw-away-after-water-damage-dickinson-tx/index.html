What to Keep, Clean or Throw Away After Water Damage in Dickinson, TX

After a loss, every wet item raises the same question. The answer depends on where the water came from, how long things stayed wet and what the item is made of.

By the Dickinson Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 13, 2026.

Call (844) 950-6900See all services

Standing in a wet living room, it is tempting to save everything or to throw out everything. Neither works. Saving contaminated items risks illness and lingering odor, while discarding salvageable ones costs money and memories. A simple method based on the water category and the material lets you sort quickly and with confidence.

Start with the water category

The category of the water decides most of the answer. Clean water from a supply line is Category 1. Water from a washer or dishwasher overflow is Category 2. Sewage, floodwater and bayou water are Category 3. Our water extraction page explains the categories in more detail.

With Category 1 water that has been wet for less than a day, many items can be dried and kept. With Category 3 water, porous items are almost always discarded, and hard items are cleaned and disinfected.

Hard and non-porous items

Glass, metal, ceramic, hard plastic and sealed stone can usually be cleaned, even after contaminated water. Wash with hot water and detergent, then disinfect and let them air dry. Solid wood furniture with a sealed finish can often be cleaned and dried, though it may warp if it stayed wet for days.

Porous and absorbent items

Carpet pad, mattresses, upholstered furniture, pillows, stuffed toys and particleboard soak up water and hold contaminants. After floodwater or sewage, these are normally thrown away. After clean water, some can be dried if the loss is caught early and the item is not sagging, stained or smelling musty.

Food, medicine and cosmetics

Throw away any food, drink, medicine or cosmetics that touched floodwater or sewage. That includes items in cardboard, plastic wrap or screw-top jars, because contaminated water can seep in. Canned goods with intact, undented seals can be washed and sanitized, but discard any that are bulging, leaking or rusted. When in doubt, throw it out.

Clothing and fabrics

Washable clothing and linens from clean water loss can go through a hot wash. After contaminated water, wash with detergent and a sanitizing additive, or take them to a dry cleaner who has been told what the items were exposed to. If a smell remains after washing, the item may not be salvageable.

Books, papers and photographs

These are fragile, and speed matters. Our companion article on saving photos and documents explains how to stabilize them. For now, separate them from other wet items and keep them cool until you can decide.

Electronics and appliances

Do not plug in anything that was wet. Have a qualified technician check appliances and electronics before use. Items that sat in floodwater are usually not worth the risk.

Flooring and building materials

Wet carpet and pad are often removed, especially after Category 2 or 3 water. Hardwood may be dried in place if the loss is clean and caught early, as the hardwood floor page explains. Insulation and drywall that stayed wet are commonly removed.

A three-pile method

  1. Keep: hard, clean-water items that you can wash and dry.
  2. Maybe: valuables and sentimental items that need a professional decision.
  3. Discard: porous items exposed to contaminated water, spoiled food and anything unsafe.

Photograph each pile before moving anything. Make a list of what is discarded, with the approximate age and value, because your insurer may ask for it.

Protect yourself while sorting

Wear rubber gloves, boots and eye protection. Use a mask if you are handling debris or moldy materials. Wash your hands thoroughly after contact with wet items, and keep children and pets out of the area.

Think about odor

Odor is a clue. An item that smells musty after cleaning probably still holds moisture or contamination. Give it a day in dry air and smell it again. If the odor persists, replace it rather than live with it.

The keep or discard rules above follow the water categories in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

A Dickinson note: documentation before disposal

The Texas Department of Insurance advises homeowners to photograph damage before any work begins, which is why you should photograph discarded items before they leave the house. For debris pickup questions, the City of Dickinson website has a Trash & Recycling page.

After Harvey in 2017, the Galveston County judge called Dickinson the hardest-hit part of the county, and KTRK reported in 2022 that the city was still rebuilding five years later, so many residents have sorted through a flooded house before.

Sources: Texas Department of Insurance. City of Dickinson website, Trash & Recycling. Galveston County judge statement on Harvey. KTRK ABC13, 2022.

Dickinson sorting questions

Can I keep a couch that got wet from a clean pipe break?

Sometimes, if it is dried quickly and does not sag or smell. Floodwater or sewage means it should go.

Do I have to keep damaged items for my adjuster?

Photograph and list them before disposal. Ask your adjuster whether they want to see large items first.

What do I do with a flooded refrigerator?

Discard the food, keep the appliance closed and ask about disposal. Do not tape it shut with food inside.

Are canned goods from a flooded kitchen safe?

Undamaged sealed cans can be washed and sanitized. Discard bulging, leaking or rusted ones.
