Kemah has a short, specific rule that matters after a loss. The city says that when repairs to a building cost 50 percent or more of its market value, whatever the cause of damage, the work needs building permits and elevation certificates, and the elevation rule extends to equipment such as an air conditioning unit.

That means a large water loss in Kemah can turn into a permitting project, so document the damage carefully from the first hour.

When water is spreading in Kemah

The city's own flood page says that regardless of where you live in Kemah you are at risk of flooding, and it publishes inundation maps from Hurricane Ike in 2008 and Hurricane Harvey in 2017. Flooding here comes from surge pushed up the bay or from heavy rain. If water is entering a Kemah building, treat it as contaminated and keep people out of it.

Close the source

Shut the main water valve if a line is leaking, and open a low faucet to ease pressure.

Cut power safely

If water touches outlets or equipment, stay out and call the electric provider or 911 before calling us.

Raise stock and papers

Move inventory, records and electronics above the waterline.

Avoid bay water

Surge water carries sewage and debris, so no one should wade in it.

Waterfront businesses and homes

The Kemah Boardwalk, with its restaurants and shops, is the town's best-known landmark and was hit hard by Ike's surge. Restaurants and retail space bring commercial losses to the page. The commercial water damage page covers containment, records and downtime. Homes along the bay face the surge side of the same storms.

Permits, water service and who to ask

The City of Kemah requires permits and inspections through its MyGov portal, and the building department is the place to ask about substantial damage. Galveston County lists WCID No. 12 as the water provider for unincorporated Kemah and Clear Lake Shores, and the city's permit checklist asks for a letter from the water or utility district of record.

If you are not sure who serves your address, ask before a repair.

Services Kemah asks for

Surge and rain flooding lead, followed by commercial and restaurant losses. The flood damage cleanup page explains contaminated water. The sewage backup cleanup page covers drains that push water up. The water extraction page covers pumping. The next bayside town south of Kemah is Bacliff.

History of storms

FEMA has described Hurricane Ike as ravaging Kemah's tourist shops, restaurants and boardwalk. Hurricane Carla damaged the town in 1961, and the 1900 Galveston hurricane leveled it. That history is why the city publishes flood protection information, and why many owners keep a record of their elevation.

Reopening a food business

A restaurant or shop that took on water usually cannot reopen the next day. Dry the floors, remove wet porous materials, sanitize surfaces and confirm with the local health authority what inspection is needed before you serve customers again. Keep dated photographs of the damage, the cleanup and the dry readings, because the insurer, the landlord and the city may each ask for them.

Ask the crew to report which areas can reopen first so you can plan service around the work.

Insurance and the elevation certificate

The Texas Department of Insurance says standard home policies exclude flood damage and that a federal flood policy requires a claim within 60 days. Keep your elevation certificate with your policy documents, since a claim or a rebuild may need it.
