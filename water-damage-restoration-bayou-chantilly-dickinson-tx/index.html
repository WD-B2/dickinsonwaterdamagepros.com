Local reporters have written about Bayou Chantilly by name. A Galveston Daily News report one year after Hurricane Harvey described it as an old neighborhood south of the city, with oak trees and dozens of single-family homes, and said it was hit particularly hard. A FOX 26 report in 2018 said Harvey rain and an overflowing Dickinson Bayou flooded the area.

What the Harvey reports mean for a water damage call

Those reports describe one storm, so they are not a forecast for your street. What they do tell a homeowner is that bayou water and heavy rain have reached homes here before, and that a flooded house is a different job from a leaking pipe.

Bayou and street water is treated as Category 3, which means it carries contamination from soil, drains and the ground it crossed. Wet drywall, insulation, carpet pad and anything porous usually comes out instead of being dried in place. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration is the reference for that sorting.

Our flood damage restoration page explains how the crew applies it to what can be saved and what cannot.

If water is moving into the house right now

Get everyone out of moving water

Do not wade through flooded yards or streets. Hidden holes, debris and live wiring are common.

Leave the panel alone if it is wet

Cut breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it standing on dry ground. Otherwise call the electric provider or 911.

Lift what you can

Raise papers, electronics and small furniture to a higher shelf or another floor.

Photograph before you move things

Take wide and close photos of the water line and the damage. Your insurer will ask for them.

Drying a house after bayou or storm water

Once the water is out, the work shifts to drying. Crews pump or extract standing water first, using the steps on the water extraction page. Then they open wet wall cavities, remove materials that cannot be saved and set air movers and dehumidifiers. The structural drying page describes how readings are taken each visit so the crew can show when the structure is dry.

Older, tree-shaded lots can hold moisture around a foundation after heavy rain. Age alone does not tell us what is behind your walls, so the crew uses moisture meters and thermal imaging rather than guessing.

Leaks and storm intrusion that are not flooding

Not every call here is a flood. Roofs lose shingles in storms, and water finds its way into ceilings and attics. Appliances and water heaters fail in any house. If your damage is a wet ceiling, see ceiling water damage repair. If a storm drove rain through a window or roofline, storm water damage cleanup covers the scope.

Insurance paperwork after a loss

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood damage, which is a separate coverage. Ask your agent which policy responds to your loss before you assume. The Texas Department of Insurance publishes guidance on filing a claim, and we do not promise coverage or an outcome.

We document the loss as the work happens. That means moisture readings, photographs of what was removed and a written scope you can hand to an adjuster. Keep your own receipts for emergency repairs and any hotel stay.

Bayou Chantilly water damage questions

Did Bayou Chantilly flood during Hurricane Harvey?

Local news reports from 2018 say Harvey rain and an overflowing Dickinson Bayou flooded the neighborhood. Whether your own home took water depends on the lot, so use your own records.

Should I wait for the water to go down before calling?

No. Call while the water is still present so the crew can plan extraction and tell you what is safe to touch. Stay out of moving water and away from wet electrical panels.

Will wet drywall have to be removed after a flood?

Often, yes. Floodwater is Category 3, so porous materials such as drywall, insulation and carpet pad are usually removed. The crew shows you the moisture readings behind that decision.

Does my homeowners policy cover flood damage?

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, which is a separate coverage. Ask your agent which policy applies, and give the adjuster our written scope and photographs.

Is this neighborhood in a FEMA flood zone?

We did not verify a neighborhood-wide status, and zones differ by lot. Look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Do you answer calls at night?

Yes. The emergency line is answered around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays.
