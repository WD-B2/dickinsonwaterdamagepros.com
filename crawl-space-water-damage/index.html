Crawl Space Water Damage in Dickinson, TX

Water under the house can soak joists, insulation and subflooring for weeks without being seen. Call to talk through crawl space water damage in Dickinson, TX.

Call (844) 950-6900See all services

Crawl space water damage in Dickinson, TX begins in a part of the house few people visit. In homes built on piers or a raised foundation, the space between the ground and the floor holds pipes, ductwork and insulation. When water enters from a leak or from outside, it can sit for days before anyone looks.

Wet crawl spaces matter for the rooms above. Moisture rises into the subfloor and floor framing, and humid air under the house can reach the living space. Drying the space is often the only way to protect the floor.

Where the water comes from

  • Supply and drain lines that leak under the floor.
  • Rain or flood water that enters through vents and low points.
  • A disconnected condensate line from an air handler.
  • Plumbing from a bathroom or kitchen directly above.
  • Poor grading that sends water toward the foundation.

Signs of a wet crawl space

Soft or bouncy floors, a musty smell, cupped hardwood, sagging insulation hanging from the floor framing and standing water or dark soil visible through an access hatch all suggest moisture. Condensation on ducts or pipes can also indicate high humidity.

Check before you enter

Do not enter a crawl space with standing water if lighting, outlets or electrical lines might be wet. Use the access hatch to look in with a flashlight first. Call your electric provider or 911 for any shock risk, and do not go in if you smell gas or sewage.

What to do first

  1. Stop any leak you can find by closing the supply valve.
  2. Photograph what you can see from the hatch.
  3. Do not stir up wet insulation, which can be heavy and contaminated.
  4. Call our line to discuss extraction and drying.

How crews dry a crawl space

Crews extract standing water with small pumps, remove wet insulation and check the subfloor and joists with moisture meters. Insulation that has absorbed water is bagged and replaced. Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up so air can circulate under the floor and along the framing.

If the water is from a storm or an outside source, it is Category 3, and porous materials are removed. If the source is a clean supply line, drying in place is more likely. The plan depends on what the water touched on its way in.

Ventilation, vapor barriers and the floor above

Once the space is dry, a vapor barrier over the soil can reduce moisture coming from below. This is a building improvement and not part of drying, and a contractor can advise on it. Flooring above a wet crawl space, especially hardwood, may need separate attention. The hardwood floor water damage page explains cupping and what to expect.

Dickinson notes

Water in a crawl space after heavy rain should lead you to check grading and drainage around the foundation as well as vents. After a major storm, the City of Dickinson Building Permits and Inspections office at 4403 Highway 3 can tell you whether repairs need permits. Ask your insurer about what your policy covers under the house.

Access and safety in a tight space

Crawl spaces are cramped, dark and often home to insects, snakes and sharp debris. Crews wear protective gear, bring lighting and use low-profile equipment. If your access hatch is small or blocked by stored items, clear it before the crew arrives. Do not crawl in yourself if the space is wet, since electrical lines, ductwork and plumbing run through it.

Describe what you see from the hatch and the crew will plan the route and the equipment.

Crawl space questions

Can I dry a crawl space myself?

Small spills sometimes. Standing water, wet insulation and subfloor moisture are better handled with pumps, meters and dehumidifiers.

Will a wet crawl space damage my floors?

It can. Moisture rises into the subfloor and can cause cupping, swelling or mold on the underside.

Do I need to replace the insulation?

Wet insulation usually has to go, because it holds water against the floor framing.

Is crawl space drying covered by insurance?

It depends on the cause. Sudden leaks are often covered and floods are not, so ask your insurer.
