How to choose the right water damage service

Water damage restoration in Dickinson, TX covers a wide range of jobs, and the right page depends on what failed or where the water came from. A burst supply line, a rusted water heater and a bayou flood all leave wet materials behind, yet each one calls for different first steps, different safety checks and a different drying plan.

Start with the cause if you know it. If you are not sure, start with the room. Ceilings, floors and walls each have their own page because drywall, hardwood and carpet react to water in different ways, and the decision to dry or replace is made material by material.

Every service below begins the same way. Dispatch takes your call around the clock, the crew confirms the water category and the affected area, and the scope and price are explained before work starts. After that, extraction, moisture mapping and drying follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

Not sure which service fits? Describe it to dispatch and they will point you to the right one.

Water categories and why they change the plan

Restoration professionals sort water into three categories. Category 1 comes from a clean source, such as a supply line or a tub overflow. Category 2 carries some contamination, such as a washing machine or dishwasher discharge. Category 3 is grossly contaminated, and it includes sewage, bayou floodwater and any water that has touched the ground outside.

Category matters because it decides what can be dried and what must be removed. Clean water can often be extracted and the materials dried in place. Category 3 water soaks into porous materials such as drywall, carpet and insulation, and those are removed rather than saved. Water that begins as Category 1 can also slide to Category 2 or 3 if it sits, which is why speed counts.

Questions about our services

Which service page should I read first after a leak?

Read the page that matches the cause, and call the 24/7 line if water is still spreading. The group headings above sort services by pipes, appliances, weather, water removal, floors and commercial property.

Do I need a different crew for sewage or flood water?

The dispatcher will ask about the water first. Sewage and floodwater are Category 3, so crews bring protective gear and treat porous materials differently. The sewage backup cleanup page explains the safety steps.

Is mold remediation part of these services?

These pages cover water damage restoration only. Fast drying is the best way to limit mold, and if you already see growth, ask the dispatcher what is available for your address.

How long does drying usually take?

Many structures need several days of equipment time, and the crew decides by moisture readings rather than a fixed schedule. See structural drying for what the readings mean.
