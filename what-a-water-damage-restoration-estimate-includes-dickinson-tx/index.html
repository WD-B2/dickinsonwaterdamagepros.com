What a Water Damage Restoration Estimate Includes in Dickinson, TX

An estimate is a plan with prices attached. Knowing what belongs in it helps you compare bids, avoid surprises and give your insurer what it needs.

By the Dickinson Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 13, 2026.

Call (844) 950-6900See all services

A restoration estimate is more than a number. Done well, it lists what will be done, in what order, with what equipment and how it will be measured. Done poorly, it is a single total with no detail. When water is in the house you may not have time to study it, but the main sections are easy to recognize.

Emergency services

This part covers the first response: assessing the damage, stopping the source if needed, protecting contents and extracting standing water. It often includes a minimum charge for the first visit and charges for labor and equipment. Our water extraction page describes what happens during that visit.

Water extraction

Extraction removes bulk water from floors, carpet and pad. The estimate may list it by area, by hour or by equipment used. Ask how the quantity is measured.

Demolition and removal

Wet materials that cannot be saved are removed: carpet pad, drywall, insulation, baseboards. The estimate should say which materials, how much and why. Photos and moisture readings should back the decision.

Drying equipment

Air movers, dehumidifiers and air scrubbers are usually charged per unit per day. The estimate should show how many units and for how long, and how that was decided. Ask how the days will be adjusted if the structure dries faster or slower than expected.

Monitoring and documentation

Moisture readings are taken each visit to show progress. The estimate should include these visits and the records they produce. Documentation helps your insurer see why the work was needed.

Cleaning and sanitizing

Surfaces and contents may need cleaning, antimicrobial treatment or deodorizing, especially after Category 2 or 3 water. The estimate should name the areas and the method.

Contents handling

Moving, packing and storing contents may appear as a separate line. Ask whether contents cleaning is included.

Rebuild and repair

If the company also rebuilds, the estimate may list drywall, paint, flooring and trim. If not, it may state the point at which the work ends. Our structural drying page explains where drying ends and repair begins.

What a good estimate leaves clear

  • The date and address of the loss.
  • The water category and source.
  • The affected rooms and measurements.
  • Line items with quantities and units.
  • Exclusions and assumptions.
  • How changes will be handled.

Why numbers change

Water damage is hard to see, so scopes can change as the crew opens walls and reads moisture. A good company explains changes before work is done and documents the reason. Be cautious if the final bill is far from the estimate and no one can explain why.

Estimates and insurance

Insurers often compare restoration estimates against their own pricing, and they may approve some items and question others. Keep your copy, the moisture logs and the invoices. If the insurer disputes an item, documentation is how you support it.

Questions to ask

  1. What is included and excluded?
  2. How is equipment time determined?
  3. What happens if more is found?
  4. When is payment due?
  5. What records will I receive?

Reading the estimate line by line

Take the estimate to a quiet table and read it twice. On the first pass, check the basics: your name, the address, the date of the loss and the cause as the company understands it. A wrong address or a wrong cause can cause trouble later with your insurer.

On the second pass, look at quantities. Square footage of removed drywall, linear feet of baseboard and number of drying units should roughly match what you saw in the house. If a number looks too high or too low, ask how it was measured.

Payment terms and approvals

The estimate should say when payment is due and what approvals the company needs before it changes the scope. Many losses are paid in part by an insurer, so ask how payment works when the insurer pays only part of the invoice. Get the answer in writing, and do not sign anything you have not read, even when the crew is waiting.

Ask for a plain-language walkthrough

A good project manager can walk you through the document in a few minutes. Ask them to explain which items protect your property right now and which items can wait. That conversation tells you a lot about how the company will treat you for the rest of the job.

A scope written to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard should name the water category, the drying goals and how progress will be measured.

What to do before you accept an estimate

  • Photograph the damage before work starts.
  • Ask for the scope in writing.
  • Do not discard damaged items until they are documented.
  • Call your insurer to report the loss.

A Dickinson note: inspections and timing

Texas insurance guidance says sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or toilet overflow, is covered, while gradual leaks are not and floods need a separate flood policy. That makes the cause stated in your estimate important. In Dickinson, same-day inspections must be scheduled by 6 a.m., a rule the city set effective July 11, 2024, so build the timing of permitted repairs into your plan.

Sources: Texas Department of Insurance. City of Dickinson Building Permits and Inspections (MyGov).

Dickinson estimate questions

Can I get a firm price over the phone?

Not for a loss no one has seen. Prices depend on measurements, moisture readings and materials.

Why do drying days vary?

Materials dry at different rates. Readings decide when the structure is dry.

Will my insurer pay the whole estimate?

That depends on your policy and the cause. The insurer decides coverage.

Can I compare two estimates?

Yes. Compare line items and assumptions, not just totals.
