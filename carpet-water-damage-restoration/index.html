Carpet Water Damage in Dickinson, TX

A soaked carpet can often be saved if the water was clean and the pad is dried fast. Call to talk through carpet water damage in Dickinson, TX.

Call (844) 950-6900See all services

Carpet water damage restoration in Dickinson, TX comes down to one question: dry or replace? Carpet itself is fairly tolerant of water, but the pad underneath acts like a sponge and the subfloor below it can stay wet long after the surface feels dry. Deciding what to save depends on the water category, the time and the carpet's construction.

Clean water caught early often allows drying in place. Water that has sat for days, or water that came from outside or a sewer, changes the answer. Moisture readings and a look at the pad tell the story.

What decides whether carpet is saved

  • Water category. Clean water from a supply line can be dried. Contaminated water means removal.
  • Time. Carpet and pad that have been wet for a day or two begin to break down and smell.
  • Pad type. Foam and rubber pads can sometimes be dried. Older fiber pads usually cannot.
  • Backing and seams. Delamination or loose seams signal damage.
  • Subfloor. Wet plywood under the pad must be dried before the carpet goes back.

What to do right away

  1. Stop the water and remove standing water.
  2. Lift furniture and put foil or blocks under the legs.
  3. Photograph the carpet and note the date and time.
  4. Do not walk on wet carpet, which pushes water deeper.
  5. Call us for extraction and drying.

How crews dry wet carpet

Crews extract water with weighted tools that press out the carpet and pad. They may lift the carpet at a corner, peel it back and pull the pad if it is not worth saving. Air movers are aimed under the lifted carpet and dehumidifiers pull the moisture from the air. A moisture meter checks the subfloor.

Carpet that is clean can be stretched back into place once the subfloor is dry. Clean carpet and a new pad is a common approach, since pad costs far less than carpet. The slab leak page describes why moisture over concrete can stay longer than expected.

Gray and black water on carpet

Water from a washing machine or a dishwasher is gray and may allow cleaning if handled quickly. Water from a sewer or flood is not safe for carpet. The flood damage cleanup page explains why porous materials are removed.

Odor and mold prevention

Wet carpet develops an odor as bacteria grow. Fast drying prevents most of it. If a smell remains after drying, the pad or subfloor may still hold moisture. Antimicrobial treatment is used on cleanable carpet, and replacement is sensible when odor persists.

Choosing replacement carpet

If replacement is needed, ask for a pad rated for your needs and check that the installer will wait until the subfloor reads dry. Carpet laid over damp plywood can trap moisture and cause the same problems again.

Carpet fibers and what they tolerate

Nylon and polyester carpets handle water well and often survive a clean-water soaking. Wool absorbs more and can shrink or stain. Looped styles, such as Berber, tend to snag when pulled and re-stretched, and cut-pile styles recover more easily. Dye bleed is another risk, especially where a wet rug or colored furniture sat on light carpet.

Take photos of the fibers and any stains, and tell the crew what the carpet is made of if you know.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration is the reference behind the rule that carpet and pad exposed to contaminated water are removed rather than cleaned.

Carpet water damage questions

How long can carpet stay wet before it must be replaced?

Clean water caught within a day or two can often be dried. After that, odor and breakdown become more likely.

Can I save the pad?

Sometimes with foam or rubber, rarely with older fiber pads.

Will carpet shrink after drying?

It may need restretching once the subfloor is dry.

Can I rent a carpet cleaner and fix it myself?

A cleaner can handle surface stains, but it does not dry the pad and subfloor.
