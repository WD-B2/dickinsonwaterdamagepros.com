What water damage restoration covers in Dickinson

Water damage restoration is the work of removing water, drying the building and repairing what the water ruined. In a Dickinson home that usually starts with extraction, then moisture readings in the walls and subfloor, then air movers and dehumidifiers running for several days. Rebuilding comes last.

The order matters because wet drywall, insulation and padding begin to break down quickly in Gulf Coast humidity. The sooner water is out and air is moving, the more of the structure stays in place. That is the difference between replacing a section of baseboard and replacing a whole room.

We work to the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, and homeowners are welcome to ask any company they call whether its crews do the same. Each job begins with a plain explanation of the scope, and scope and price are explained before work starts.

The first 72 hours

What to do in the first 24 to 72 hours

Mold needs moisture and time. These four steps shorten both, and the first two can be done before anyone arrives.

  1. Stop the source

    Shut off the main water valve if a pipe or fixture is running. If water is near outlets or panels, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.

  2. Call the 24/7 line

    Tell dispatch what failed, how far the water has spread and whether the water looks clean, gray or dirty.

  3. Document the loss

    Take wide and close photos and a short video before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them.

  4. Start extraction and drying

    Crews remove standing water, take moisture readings and set drying equipment in the affected rooms.

Why water damage keeps happening in Dickinson

Dickinson sits on the mainland side of Galveston County, and Dickinson Bayou runs through town on its way to Dickinson Bay. Gum Bayou and other tributaries cross the city as well. When heavy rain stalls over the Gulf Freeway corridor, those channels fill, and streets such as FM 517 and Highway 3 can go under water before the bayou crests.

Hurricane Harvey in August 2017 set the local benchmark. The National Weather Service reported that many locations received over three feet of rain, with totals over 40 inches common, and the Galveston County judge called Dickinson the hardest-hit part of the county. More recently, KTRK reported the bayou spilling its banks in July 2024 after more than six inches of rain.

Not every loss comes from a storm. Supply lines fail, water heaters rust through, air conditioner drain pans overflow in closets and washing machine hoses let go while nobody is home. Those are the calls that arrive on an ordinary Tuesday, and the first hours matter just as much.

Floodwater and a supply line leak are different problems. Floodwater from a bayou is treated as contaminated and brings a different cleanup plan, which our flood damage cleanup page explains.

What affects the cost of water damage restoration

Price follows the work, and the work follows the water. We explain scope and price before work starts, and we do not quote a number before someone has looked at the structure. These are the factors that move a restoration job up or down.

  • Water category. Clean supply water, gray water and sewage or floodwater need different handling, and Category 3 water means removing porous materials rather than drying them.
  • Square footage and rooms affected. More wet area means more extraction time and more equipment.
  • Materials. Drywall, carpet pad and insulation are usually cheaper to replace than to dry. Hardwood and plaster often are not.
  • Time since the loss. Water that sat for days reaches deeper into the wall cavity than water caught in the first day.
  • Equipment days. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the dry standard, not for a set number of days.
  • Rebuild scope. Drying and rebuilding are separate phases, and some jobs only need the first.

Insurance in plain terms

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner and renter policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, toilet overflow or broken washer hose, and usually do not cover gradual leaks. Home policies do not cover floods, which need a separate flood policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer before you assume either way.

Questions Dickinson homeowners ask first

How fast should I call after water damage starts?

Call as soon as you have stopped the source and it is safe to be in the room. Drywall and padding absorb water within hours, and Dickinson humidity slows natural drying. Early extraction usually means fewer materials to replace.

What does water damage restoration cost in Dickinson?

It depends on the water category, the number of rooms, the materials involved and how long the water sat. We explain scope and price before work starts, after someone has seen the damage. A small kitchen leak and a flooded first floor are very different jobs.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage?

Often it covers sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or broken hose, and often it does not cover gradual leaks. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs a separate flood policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

What should I document for an insurance claim?

Photograph and video every wet room before cleanup, list damaged belongings, keep receipts for emergency work and save any parts that failed, such as a cracked hose. Do not discard damaged items until your adjuster has seen them.

Do you handle large losses with several rooms or a whole floor?

Yes. Large losses use truck-mounted or submersible extraction, more drying equipment and a written moisture log. The same 24/7 line coordinates it, and the structural drying page explains how long drying takes.

Can you fix a leak or waterproof my basement or foundation?

Restoration repairs damage after water gets in. Finding and fixing a leaking pipe is a plumber's job, and permanent waterproofing is a separate trade. We can tell you what we see so you know which professional to call next.

Do you cover areas outside Dickinson?

Yes. We dispatch to League City, Bacliff, Santa Fe, Kemah and Webster, and the dispatcher can confirm other nearby addresses.
