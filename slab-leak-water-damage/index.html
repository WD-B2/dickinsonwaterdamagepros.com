Warm spot on the floor or a bill that keeps climbing?

If one patch of floor feels warm, if you hear water running with every tap closed or if the water bill rises with no change in use, a line under the foundation may be leaking. Plumbers call this a slab leak, and restoration crews call the result slab leak water damage. Once water reaches the surface, it becomes a drying problem as well as a plumbing one.

Slab leak water damage in Dickinson, TX happens when a supply or drain line beneath a concrete slab fails and water finds its way up through cracks, joints and the edges of the slab. The water may surface at one corner or travel across the whole floor under the flooring.

How a slab leak shows itself

  • A hot spot on tile or wood where a hot-water line is leaking.
  • The sound of running water when everything is off.
  • A water meter that moves after fixtures are closed.
  • Damp carpet, lifted flooring or cracks in the grout.
  • A musty smell along baseboards.

When a slab leak is an emergency

Water at the surface means the leak is already moving through your floor. It is urgent when the leak is supply pressure, because the line will keep flowing until it is shut off. A plumber needs to confirm the source, and you can close the main water valve to slow it in the meantime.

Water and electricity

Floors with standing water can carry shock hazards if outlets or appliances are involved. Stay out of the room if water is near electrical equipment, and cut power to that area only if you can reach the panel dry. Call your electric provider or 911 for any shock risk before calling the restoration line.

What to do right away

  1. Close the main water valve and see if the meter stops moving.
  2. Move belongings off wet flooring and photograph the damage.
  3. Call a plumber with leak detection equipment to locate the line.
  4. Call our 24/7 line for extraction and drying.

Drying a floor over a slab

The structure under your flooring is concrete, which dries slowly and holds moisture longer than wood. Crews extract surface water, then use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find where the slab and flooring are wet. Carpet and pad are lifted and dried or removed, laminate and wood flooring is often removed, and tile may be saved if the subfloor is dried. Concrete can need extended dehumidification.

Because water can move under the slab and surface elsewhere, the wet area is not always where the leak is. Readings are repeated to confirm that the slab is dry before new flooring goes down. The structural drying page covers how readings work.

What restoration does not do

Restoration removes water and dries materials. It does not fix the pipe under the slab. Plumbers repair slab leaks by rerouting the line or breaking into the concrete, and they will tell you which approach fits. Doing the drying before the repair, or at least in step with it, keeps you from rebuilding over a wet floor.

If a major repair is planned, ask the City of Dickinson Building Permits and Inspections office at 4403 Highway 3 whether a permit applies.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration guides how flooring over a slab is dried, including the moisture readings that show when the floor is ready for repair.

Slab leak questions

Why is there a warm spot on my floor, and is it a slab leak?

A warm spot can mean a hot-water line is leaking under the slab. Close the main valve, check whether the meter stops and have a plumber test the line. If water has reached the floor, call us for drying.

Do you handle slab leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Dickinson?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open at all hours, and crews are dispatched by address and loss size. Same-day service is the goal when crews are available.

Can the flooring be saved?

Tile often can. Carpet may be dried, and laminate or wood is commonly replaced.

Does insurance cover a slab leak?

Sometimes. Coverage depends on your policy and the cause, so ask your insurer.
