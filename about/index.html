What Dickinson Water Damage Pros does

Dickinson Water Damage Pros is a 24/7 water damage line for Dickinson, Texas and the Galveston Bay towns around it. You call one number, you describe what happened and a licensed and insured restoration crew is dispatched to the property. The work covers water removal, structural drying and documentation, and it ranges from a single leaking appliance to a flooded first floor.

We started from a simple observation about water damage calls. People rarely have time to compare companies when water is running through a ceiling. They need someone to answer, explain what will happen and say what it is likely to involve before anyone starts moving furniture. That is the standard this site is built around.

How a call works

Dispatch asks what failed, where the water is and how long it has been there. Those answers decide which crew goes and what equipment loads onto the truck. If the water is near electrical equipment or looks like sewage, the dispatcher gives safety steps first, because those situations should not be handled with a mop.

On arrival, the crew checks the water category and uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find wet areas that are not obvious from the surface. Then you hear the scope and the price, and work starts only after that conversation. We do not quote prices by phone for a loss no one has seen.

Standards we work to

Professional water damage restoration has a published standard, ANSI/IICRC S500. It describes water categories, drying goals and documentation practices, and we encourage homeowners to ask any company whether its crews work to it. Our crews are licensed and insured. We do not claim awards, rankings or review scores, and we do not publish numbers we cannot back up.

Why a local focus matters

Dickinson has dealt with serious water. Dickinson Bayou crosses town, Gum Bayou and other tributaries feed it, and Hurricane Harvey in 2017 left much of the city flooded. That history shapes how we think about a call. A kitchen leak in a slab house on one street and a bayou flood on another are different problems, and the people who answer the phone know it.

We also keep the service area honest. The nearby towns we list have their own pages because their utilities, permit offices and flood histories differ. If an address is outside what we can reach, dispatch says so instead of promising a visit.

What you see on site

The crew starts by introducing who is on the job and who to call with questions. They walk the affected rooms with you, point out what is wet and mark the areas that will be opened up. Furniture and belongings are moved or protected before extraction begins, and the crew tells you what is being removed and why.

Each visit adds readings to a log. The log shows where moisture started, how it changed and when the structure reached its drying goal. Photographs of the damage and the equipment are taken along the way. These records help the work stay on track, and they give your insurer a clear account of what happened.

What we ask of you

We ask for a few things in return. Tell the dispatcher honestly what you know about the cause, even if it is embarrassing. Keep damaged items until they are photographed. Leave equipment running while the crew is away, because stopping it early slows drying and extends the job.

Records you can ask for

At the end of a job you can ask for a copy of the moisture log, the photographs and the equipment record. These are the same documents we keep for ourselves, so there is nothing extra to produce. Many homeowners send them straight to their adjuster, and they also help if the property is sold later and a buyer asks about past water damage.

What we do not do

We do not provide mold services through this site, and we do not offer plumbing repair or permanent waterproofing. If a pipe has to be replaced or a drainage problem has to be solved, we tell you what we found so you can hire the right trade. We do not promise insurance outcomes either. We document the loss clearly, and the claim decision belongs to your insurer.
