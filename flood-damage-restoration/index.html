Emergency Flood Damage Cleanup in Dickinson, TX

Flood water in a Dickinson home is an emergency and is treated as contaminated. Call our 24/7 line, stay out of the water and read the safety steps below.

Call (844) 950-6900See all services

Flood damage restoration in Dickinson, TX deals with water that arrives from outside, whether from Dickinson Bayou, Gum Bayou, a clogged street drain or a storm surge pushed up Galveston Bay. Flood water differs from a plumbing leak in one important way. It has been on the ground, in ditches and sometimes in sewers, so restoration treats it as Category 3 contaminated water.

That one fact changes the plan. Porous materials such as drywall, insulation, carpet and pad that have been soaked by flood water are generally removed, not dried in place. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and sanitized, then dried. The result is a larger tear-out than a clean-water leak, and it is why speed and safe handling matter so much.

Dickinson's flood history

Dickinson has deep experience with flooding that it would rather not repeat. The National Weather Service reported that many locations received over three feet of rain during Hurricane Harvey in 2017, with totals over 40 inches common, and the Galveston County judge called Dickinson the hardest-hit part of the county. KTRK later reported that the town was still rebuilding five years afterward.

Floods do not require a hurricane. In July 2024 KTRK reported that Dickinson Bayou spilled its banks after more than six inches of rain, stranding drivers and neighbors. Heavy rain, a high tide and a blocked drain can combine to put water into a house that has never flooded before.

When flood water is an emergency

Any water entering from outside is urgent. So is water that has reached electrical outlets, a furnace or air handler, or the lower portion of the walls. Do not enter a flooded room if the breaker panel is wet or if there is a gas smell.

Stay out of flood water

Flood water can carry sewage, chemicals and debris, and it can hide live electrical hazards. Do not wade through it, do not use household vacuums on it and keep children and pets away. Call 911 for life-threatening conditions and your electric provider if lines or panels are involved, then call our 24/7 line.

What happens after you call

Crews arrive in protective gear. They extract the standing water with pumps, then remove saturated materials. Drywall is typically cut above the water line, carpet and pad are removed and wet insulation is bagged. Remaining surfaces get an antimicrobial cleaning, and framing is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Flood damage and insurance

The Texas Department of Insurance says standard home policies do not cover floods and that flood coverage is a separate policy. It also says federal flood policies require a claim and documentation within 60 days. Check the flood zone for your address on the FEMA flood map and read your policy. Photograph everything before cleanup, because removed materials will not be there for the adjuster to see later.

Rebuilding and permits

After a major flood, local rules can apply to repairs. FEMA advises flood survivors to consult local building officials about permits. In Dickinson, the Building Permits and Inspections office at 4403 Highway 3 is the place to ask. Keep records of what was removed and when it was dried so rebuilding can proceed without guessing.

Flood damage questions

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Dickinson?

Yes. The line is open 24/7 and the dispatcher sends crews by address and loss size. During a widespread flood, many homes need help at once, so call early and describe the water depth.

Can flood-soaked drywall be dried instead of removed?

Generally no. Flood water is contaminated, so porous materials that absorbed it are removed. Framing and hard surfaces can be cleaned and dried.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Standard home policies usually do not. Flood coverage is separate, and your agent can tell you whether you have it.

Is it safe to stay in the house?

Not while the water is inside and the electrical system may be affected. Follow local emergency guidance and do not re-enter until the power situation is safe.
