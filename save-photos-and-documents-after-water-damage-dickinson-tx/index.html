How to Save Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Dickinson, TX

Paper and photographs can often be saved if you act within a day or two. This guide covers what to do first, what to avoid and when to hand them to a specialist.

By the Dickinson Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 13, 2026.

Call (844) 950-6900See all services

Wet photos, certificates, tax records and family papers feel impossible to replace. The good news is that many can be saved, particularly if you work quickly. Paper becomes weaker and more likely to stick, warp or grow mold with every hour it stays wet. A calm, steady routine gives you the best chance.

First, check the water

If the items were wet by clean water, handling is safer. If floodwater or sewage touched them, wear gloves and treat the items as contaminated. Some items from Category 3 water are better left to a specialist or accepted as a loss, especially if they are not irreplaceable.

Work within the first day or two

Mold can start in one to two days in warm, humid conditions. Southeast Texas is warm and humid, so do not wait. If you cannot deal with the items right away, keep them cool and out of sun, and consider freezing them, which stops further damage until you have time.

Photographs

  • Rinse mud gently with clean, cool water. Do not rub.
  • Separate stuck photos by holding them under water and easing them apart.
  • Lay them face up on a clean, absorbent surface such as paper towels, away from direct sun.
  • Do not use hair dryers or heat.
  • If you have many, freeze them between sheets of wax paper.

Digital prints and older prints differ. Old photographs and negatives can be fragile, so handle by the edges.

Documents and books

Remove paper from wet binders and folders. Blot gently, then interleave pages with clean paper towels or unprinted newsprint. Change the interleaving paper as it gets wet. A fan blowing across the room (not directly on the items) speeds drying. Books can be stood on end with pages fanned. Very wet or large volumes are often better frozen.

Freezing as a pause button

Freezing stops mold and gives you time. Place items in freezer bags with wax paper between layers, and put them in a home or commercial freezer. A document recovery specialist can then vacuum freeze-dry them. This is the best option for large collections or irreplaceable items.

Sort what is worth saving

Prioritize irreplaceable items: photos, legal documents, family records. Passports, birth certificates and titles can usually be replaced, so do not spend hours on them if time is short. Make a list of what was lost. Your insurer may ask for it, and the list helps when you request replacements.

Digitize what you can

When items are dry, scan or photograph them. Store copies in the cloud and with a trusted relative. Next time you are prepared.

Insurance papers

Keep your policy, claim number, adjuster contact details and receipts together in a dry, safe place. The flood damage restoration page explains how the structure is dried while you focus on belongings, and the water extraction page covers the first step.

What not to do

  • Do not peel stuck pages or photos apart dry.
  • Do not use a microwave, an oven or a hair dryer.
  • Do not press wet paper between heavy books.
  • Do not leave wet items in a sealed plastic bag at room temperature.

Setting up a drying station

If you decide to dry items yourself, set up a dedicated spot away from the wet area. Use a table covered with clean paper towels or unprinted newsprint, and keep the room cool with a fan moving air across the space. Avoid direct sunlight because it can fade and curl photographs.

Work in small batches, and label each batch with the date and where the items came from. Check batches every few hours and replace damp blotting paper with dry sheets. Patience matters here, since rushing can tear fragile pages.

When to stop and call a specialist

If you have boxes of wet records, a large library or items with real historical or financial value, stop and call a document recovery specialist. They have vacuum freeze-drying equipment that a household cannot match. Freezing the items first keeps your options open while you arrange it.

A Dickinson note: the 60-day flood claim window

The Texas Department of Insurance says that a federal flood insurance claim must be filed and documented within 60 days, so records, receipts and lists of damaged belongings matter. Home policies do not cover flood, which needs a separate flood policy, so know which policy applies to your loss.

Dickinson document and photo questions

How soon should I freeze wet photos?

Within a day or two if you cannot dry them. Freezing stops mold and buys time.

Can I save photos that sat in floodwater?

Often yes, if they are rinsed gently with clean water and dried promptly. Irreplaceable items may be worth a specialist.

Should I throw away wet paperwork?

Not before listing and photographing it. Many documents can be replaced, and your insurer may want a record.

Where do I keep claim documents?

In a dry, waterproof folder with copies stored digitally.
