Storm water damage cleanup in Dickinson, TX deals with rain that gets into a building through the roof, windows, doors or walls. Storms on the upper Texas coast bring wind-driven rain, and water can enter at points that stay dry in ordinary weather, such as ridge vents, flashing, window frames and garage doors.

Storm damage is different from flood damage. Rain that comes through a roof is generally treated as cleaner than flood water that has crossed the ground, though it can pick up insulation, dust and debris on its way down. A storm that brings both rain and rising water, as Harvey did, creates both situations in one house.

What a storm leak looks like

Ceiling stains, dripping light fixtures, wet attic insulation, water running down walls, swollen window trim and damp carpet near exterior doors all point to storm entry. The stain may not show until a day or two after the storm, when insulation saturates and finally lets water through.

Storm hazards

Do not climb onto a wet roof, and stay away from downed power lines. If water is dripping through a light fixture or near outlets, stay out of the room and call your electric provider or 911 for any shock risk before calling the restoration line.

First steps after a storm

  1. Make sure everyone is safe and the weather has passed.
  2. Photograph roof damage, openings and wet areas before cleanup.
  3. Place buckets and move belongings away from drips.
  4. Cover roof openings with a tarp if a roofer can do it safely, or call one.
  5. Call our 24/7 line for extraction and drying.

Drying wind-driven rain damage

Crews extract standing water, then trace the moisture path with a thermal camera and meters. Wet attic insulation is removed, ceiling drywall is cut open where it is soft and wet carpet and pad are dried or replaced. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the cavities. The ceiling water damage repair page covers sagging and stained drywall in detail.

Roof repair is a separate trade. Ask the roofer to photograph the opening and the repair, because those photos support the insurance claim and show when the opening was closed.

Local storm history

Dickinson has seen water from tropical weather before. The National Weather Service reported over three feet of rain at many locations during Hurricane Harvey in 2017, with totals over 40 inches common. That storm combined roof leaks with rising water. Texas Department of Insurance guidance says some policies cover rain damage that entered through a wind-created opening, and standard policies do not cover flooding from rising water.

Keeping the claim organized

Wind-driven rain claims often turn on whether the opening was created by wind or hail. Document the roof damage as well as the interior water. Save estimates from the roofer, receipts for temporary repairs and the restoration crew's moisture log. If you also have flood coverage, tell that insurer separately.

Windows, doors and garage openings

Wind-driven rain often enters around window frames, under exterior doors and through garage door seals. Look for water trails on sills, swollen trim, damp carpet near doors and wet drywall under windows. These points can be sealed or flashed once the storm has passed. Photograph each entry point before repairs, because your insurer may ask which opening let the water in.

A contractor can fix the opening and the restoration crew dries the room.

What to do before help arrives

  • Stay out of rooms where water touches outlets, cords or appliances.
  • Do not wade through standing water of unknown source.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything.
  • Call 911 for downed lines or any smell of gas.

Storm water damage questions

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Dickinson?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes calls at any hour, and crews are dispatched by address and loss size. After a widespread storm many homes need help at once, so call early. Same-day response is the goal when crews are available.

Is rain through my roof covered by insurance?

Some policies cover rain damage that enters through a wind-created opening. Check your policy.

Should I tarp the roof first?

A professional should do it, and only when it is safe. Do not climb a wet roof.

Can wet attic insulation stay?

Wet insulation holds moisture against the ceiling and is usually removed.
