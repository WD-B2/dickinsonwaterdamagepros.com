Questions to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company in Dickinson, TX

When water is in the house, you may have only minutes to choose. These questions help you tell a prepared company from a pushy one, and they take a short phone call.

By the Dickinson Water Damage Pros team. Updated November 13, 2026.

Call (844) 950-6900See all services

Choosing a water damage restoration company is hard because you are choosing under pressure. The wrong choice can mean slow drying, poor records and conflicts with your insurer. A few questions on the first call can show you a lot. This list is meant to be used during that call.

Do your crews follow the industry standard?

The published standard for professional water damage restoration is ANSI/IICRC S500. Ask whether the crew works to it, and what that means for how they classify water, set drying goals and document the job. A company that cannot answer simply is a warning sign.

Are you licensed and insured?

Ask for proof of insurance, and ask which licenses apply to the work they will do. A reputable company is willing to share a certificate. Do not accept vague answers, since uninsured work on your property can become your problem.

What happens when you arrive?

You want to hear about assessment first: finding the source, classifying the water and using moisture meters and thermal cameras. A company that starts moving furniture before looking at the damage is working backward.

Will you explain the scope and price before work starts?

Ask for a written scope that separates extraction, drying, demolition and rebuilding. Ask how equipment is charged and how monitoring is handled. Prices quoted over the phone for a loss no one has seen are guesses.

What records will I get?

Insurers often want moisture logs, photographs, a list of removed materials and equipment records. Ask what the company provides and when. Good documentation is built during the job, not reconstructed afterward.

What equipment will you use?

Look for plain answers about extraction units, air movers, dehumidifiers and moisture meters. Ask how many units they expect to place and how they decide when drying is complete. The answer should refer to readings, not to a fixed number of days.

How do you handle contaminated water?

If your loss involves sewage or floodwater, ask about protective gear, disposal of removed materials and cleaning of surfaces. Our sewage backup cleanup page describes the approach.

Do you work with my insurance company?

Companies can document the loss and communicate with adjusters, but they should not promise a claim outcome. Be wary of anyone who says your claim will be approved or who asks you to sign over your claim benefits before the work is done.

Who will be on site?

Ask whether the crew is employees or subcontractors and who your point of contact will be. You should know who to call when you have a question at night.

What happens after drying?

Ask whether the company also rebuilds, or whether you will need a separate contractor. Make sure the scope is clear on where the work ends.

Red flags

  • Pressure to sign right away.
  • No explanation of the water category.
  • Vague answers about insurance.
  • A request for full payment up front.
  • No mention of moisture readings.

Compare, but quickly

If time allows, call two or three companies and compare answers. If water is spreading, pick the one that answers clearly and start. Our water extraction page describes what a competent first visit looks like.

Questions about the first hour

Ask what the company will do in the first hour after arriving and who will be there. A prepared crew can describe the order of work: safety check, source control, assessment, extraction, then equipment. Listen for specifics rather than slogans.

Questions about communication

Ask how you will receive updates and who answers the phone after hours. Water damage jobs run for days, and you will have questions. A company that gives you a direct contact and a plan for updates is easier to work with than one that routes every question through a general line.

A note on your own records

While you talk to companies, keep a simple log of names, times and what each one told you. If two companies give different answers about the same issue, the log helps you decide whom to trust. It also helps your insurer if a dispute arises later.

A Dickinson note: scams after a storm

After major storms, scammers target homeowners. Texas Law Help's disaster manual says that anyone who claims to work for the government, the Texas Department of Insurance or your insurer and asks for money to speed up a claim is a red flag. In Dickinson, the City Building Permits and Inspections office at 4403 Highway 3 is the place to ask which repairs need a permit before you hire anyone.

Sources: Texas Law Help disaster manual, insurance issues. City of Dickinson Building Permits and Inspections (MyGov).

Dickinson hiring questions

Should I get several quotes after a flood in Dickinson?

If time allows, compare two or three. When water is rising, call one reputable company and start.

Can a company bill my insurer directly?

Arrangements vary, and your insurer decides coverage. Be cautious about signing over claim rights.

Is a deposit normal?

Some jobs involve one. Ask for terms in writing, and be careful with requests for full payment before work.

How do I verify insurance?

Ask for a certificate of insurance and, if you like, contact the insurer to confirm it is active.
